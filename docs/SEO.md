# How We Play · Search and AI discoverability

**Technical/editorial audit updated 20 October 2026.** Source of truth is the current GitHub code and the public publishing model, not older launch notes. The public site could not be fetched from the external web inspection tool during this audit, so live Google/Bing indexation, real search impressions, HTTP responses and Core Web Vitals **are not independently verified here**.

## Our strategy

**Be the useful, citable, clearly identifiable source when a grassroots coach needs to understand a football format, formation or player decision.**

How We Play is the publication. Each complete formation is an **issue**. Individual tactical principles and position lessons are chapters inside an issue. We will not create thin standalone pages to chase keywords or put unreviewed coaching advice into search results.

Priority order:

1. Make it easy for crawlers, AI search and coaches to **find** complete issues.
2. Make the pages **understandable** without needing JavaScript or a visual interpretation of an SVG.
3. Show the **author, reviewer and review status** accurately and link to the editorial standards.
4. Make the **next useful action** obvious: open the issue, view a role, try a tactical illustration, and later download a free playbook.
5. Understand what actually drives **useful discovery**, not vanity rankings or speculative AI scores.

Official Google guidance: [AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) and [optimizing for generative AI in Search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide). Bing's [Webmaster Guidelines](https://www.bing.com/webmasters/help/bing-webmaster-guidelines-30fba23a) emphasise crawlability, clear content and authority.

There is **no special schema or llms.txt file required for Google AI Overviews or AI Mode**. We should not sell or represent such techniques as ranking guarantees. Search engines and AI services can still choose not to index or cite a page.

## Changes implemented in the 20 October 2026 search-discovery branch

**1. Stronger and less repetitive meta titles.** Individual playing principles and position pages use unique formation-specific titles without repeating the formation notation multiple times. Main issue titles centre the formation and 9v9 intent.

**2. Linked structured data grounded in public content.**

- Homepage: stable `WebSite`, `Organization` and author `Person` identities, with links to the real social accounts.
- 9v9 landing page: `CollectionPage` with an `ItemList` of complete, publishable issues.
- Each formation issue: `Article` and `WebPage`, with its real URL, free-access statement, verified author/reviewer metadata, published date, collection relationship and links to every available chapter.
- Each rule and role page: `Article` and `WebPage`, linked back to the exact parent issue, with descriptive headlines and grounded copy.
- `dateModified` is used **only** where a significant modification has actually been recorded as `issue.updated`, `rule.updated` or `role.updated`.
- `BreadcrumbList` remains a consistent separate block for inner pages.

Structured data helps machines **interpret** pages. It isn't a claim that the page is eligible for every Google rich result.

**3. Better XML sitemap.** `sitemap.xml` includes only public, self-canonical indexable HTML URLs. `lastmod` comes from editorial dates on the actual issue/lesson/position, or a known privacy-notice update. No invented current-build dates, changefreq or priority claims.

**4. An issue-only publication feed.** `/feed.xml` is advertised through an RSS alternate link on public pages and contains complete published issues only. Individual lessons and role pages do not become separate homepage/feed items. Preview builds do not advertise or publish this feed.

**5. Automated SEO regressions.** In `npm test`, `scripts/test-seo.mjs` verifies identities, page relationships, publication dates, review names, current URL links, issue-only feeds and XML escaping. Build-time checks also reject malformed JSON-LD, duplicate titles, absent dates for published articles and mismatched canonical/Article links. Existing gate and broken-link tests remain.

## Launch checklist · Account holder

### A. Google Search Console

- Verify a **Domain property** for `howweplayfootball.com` via the Cloudflare DNS TXT record (covers http/https and www/apex).
- Submit `https://howweplayfootball.com/sitemap.xml` under *Sitemaps*.
- Use URL Inspection on the homepage, `/formations/9v9/`, all three formation issues and representative principles/positions.
- Inspect indexing states, canonical selected by Google, crawled content, referring pages and page-experience reports. Fix actual reported problems before trying speculative tactics.
- Review Search Console **Search results** by pages, queries and non-branded traffic. Review any generative AI reporting where available; do not assume it attributes all AI traffic.

### B. Bing Webmaster Tools

- Verify/import the site and submit the same sitemap.
- Review Index Explorer, crawl issues and Bing organic queries.
- Consider **Cloudflare Crawler Hints / IndexNow** as a low-maintenance way to notify supported engines of added or materially changed public URLs. Verify activation and submissions in Bing Webmaster Tools. There is no need to build a second, hard-coded URL inventory just for IndexNow.
- IndexNow **does not guarantee** crawling or indexing.

### C. Live-site verification (not yet performed in this branch)

- Confirm `https://howweplayfootball.com/sitemap.xml` returns HTTP 200, valid XML and no `noindex` or unreviewed URLs.
- Confirm `/robots.txt` points to the sitemap and allows normal crawling.
- Confirm `/feed.xml` is valid and lists complete issues only.
- Verify http→https and www→apex redirects preserve the intended canonical host.
- Check response headers for `X-Robots-Tag`, content type and caching on production vs private preview.
- Check `/moving-up/` redirect and 404 behaviour.
- Validate a representative public Article / WebPage / BreadcrumbList in a structured data validator. Run Google's Rich Results Test only for markup it actually supports.
- Check that the privacy notice's published contact address actually receives mail. `content/site.json` currently lists `hello@howweplayfootball.com`, but the domain mailbox has not yet been confirmed; the Contact form implementation is in a **separate, unmerged** pull request. An undeliverable contact address is a visitor trust and privacy-support problem, not something structured data can fix.
- Test mobile page speed, CLS/LCP/INP and font loading with PageSpeed Insights and field data once traffic is sufficient.
- Confirm page text and links still work with scripting disabled.
- Compare sitemap URLs with Search Console indexed pages every month.

## Editorial content strategy: where we'll win

**Lead with helpful intent rather than a keyword spreadsheet.** Examples of real coaching jobs and likely search phrasing (not verified volume forecasts):

| Reader's question | Primary owned destination | Supporting content |
|---|---|---|
| How do I play 3-1-3-1 in 9v9 football? | 3-1-3-1 issue | Four principles, positions, team relationships |
| Is 3-3-2 a good 9v9 formation for two strikers? | 3-3-2 issue | Two-forward responsibilities, midfield support, trade-offs |
| How do wing-backs work in a 2-2-3-1 in 9v9? | 2-2-3-1 issue | Wing-back and defender roles, pressing/recovery; clearly an alternative |
| What changes from 7v7 to 9v9? | Bottom of 9v9 format landing page | Verified pitch, ball, goals, offside and other rule explanations |
| What does a holding midfielder do in a 3-1-3-1? | The Screen position page | Middle First and related principle |
| How should I explain new 9v9 positions to children? | Relevant issue's positional guides | Player-facing cues, diagrams and the actual responsibilities |

Don't make separate SEO pages repeating the same paragraphs for every wording variation. Existing chapter pages need **genuine distinct advice**, not synonyms of the issue introduction.

### Review-led content expansion · Not yet authorised to publish

The next editorial step is to make each issue a more helpful *reference* as well as a visual lesson:

- A short, **answer-first explanation** of what the formation is, which players operate in which lines and what its key decisions involve. Adapt only from already approved material.
- A practical **“when it can work / what to watch”** section based on real examples and coaching review, not generic promises about superiority.
- Where practical, a **coach-facing example** and a genuinely child-friendly instruction, not just a picture.
- The key **age or rules context** verified against the actual season and competition, with clear source and checked date.
- A natural internal link to the next question the coach would ask, not a mechanical list of keywords.
- PDF releases and, later, selected original short videos. Accompany videos with captions and written explanation, and only add video schema when a real watchable video is embedded on the page.
- Once 7v7 and 5v5 guides are ready, publish their actual issues and only then make those format hubs fully indexable.

New tactical claims, suggested exercises or illustrative movements require David Wilson's coaching review under the existing system. An SEO pass never bypasses the publishing gate.

## Discovery beyond search engines

- Give each formation issue its own distinctive shareable editorial cover / OG image using the existing pitch diagrams. The current social image is shared across most pages.
- Build genuine links through coaching networks, grassroots club resources, coaches' newsletters and social videos that send readers to the corresponding *free official issue*. Do not pay for inauthentic references or manufacture citations.
- Add a meaningful author/reviewer byline and a clear *last checked / reviewed* context wherever it improves trust; don't imply football clubs or authorities endorse the site.
- Consider a one-page, easy-to-share PDF only after the issue is approved. Keep the web version accessible and freely crawlable.
- Avoid pop-ups that block editorial content or forced signups before a guide.

## Measurement: first 90 days

| When | Focus | Measures |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Discovery and indexing | Sitemap acceptance, canonical selection, crawl/index status of each published page, errors |
| Weeks 3–6 | Query relevance | Branded vs non-branded impressions/clicks, query groups by formation/format, pages receiving first impressions |
| Weeks 7–12 | Reader engagement and return | Organic visits to issues, role and lesson navigation, relevant newsletter signups and repeat readers |
| Quarterly | Qualitative AI visibility | Repeat a small set of realistic discovery questions across search/AI tools; record whether and how the site is cited, without claiming sampling equals market share |

Create a simple baseline now and annotate major content releases in analytics. A target like “top three for 9v9” is premature without actual query and competitor data.

## Maintenance rules

1. New or substantially revised **coaching content** → reviewer sign-off for exact content version → issue/lesson/role editorial updated date when appropriate.
2. Only complete issues enter homepage **Latest Issues** and RSS; lesson pages remain discoverable within their issue and XML sitemap.
3. Verified rule facts must be checked each season; do not change `checkedOn` just because another page is rebuilt.
4. Broken-link, metadata and sitemap tests run for every PR and production build.
5. Review which pages Google actually indexes and improve their usefulness before publishing more pages.
6. There is **no guarantee** that any crawler, AI service or search engine will index, cite or rank How We Play. Our standard is to publish the clearest, most accurate resource we can make.

## 23 October 2026 · Branded images, formations and AI/search QA

This implementation retains the existing stable formation URLs, coaching sign-off and Mailchimp integration while improving the content users and crawlers actually see.

### Public-facing editorial information

- The homepage explicitly features the reviewed **9v9 3-1-3-1** guide, independently of publication recency. Other published formations follow as complete guides.
- Formations are now presented as **formation guides** rather than confusing, repeated "Issue 01" and "Issue 02" labels across formats; internal issue ordering is preserved for editorial workflows.
- Sitewide and homepage links use the verified Instagram, TikTok and YouTube URLs from `content/site.json`. Organization structured data already references these through `sameAs`.
- **Mailchimp remains the email platform.** Newsletter actions, double opt-in and privacy information were not replaced with Substack.

### Images and attribution

- The SVG tactical renderer contains a compact How We Play vector mark and brand attribution **inside each actual tactical image**. This discourages casual unattributed reuse and survives screenshots and exports, but **is not copy protection**; it can be removed by someone deliberately editing the image.
- Source logo's three-node geometry is used, and approved coaching diagrams are not modified.
- `scripts/og/generate.mjs` derives **60 real 1200 × 630 PNG page previews** from the *approved public model* via the existing pitch renderer, using the brand palette. There are unique images for each published formation, principle, position, format hub, homepage and About page. No draft image is generated.
- `src/layouts/Base.astro` serves route-specific `og:image`, size, alt, Twitter image, canonical, description and large-image-preview instructions.
- Public `Article` and `WebPage` structured data references the matching route-specific `ImageObject`; `CollectionPage` and homepage `WebSite` also link to their corresponding images.
- `sitemap.xml` includes `image:image` entries for indexable routes with a unique, relevant preview. These URLs are generated from the same approved routing model, not maintained by hand.
- The generic OG image remains as a fallback for non-coaching pages such as Privacy and the 404. A fallback image is valid when there is no editorially specific visual. The Google requirement is **not** that every page must have a different image; relevance helps when an image genuinely adds value.

### Automated assurance

`scripts/test-seo.mjs` checks for unique publication-gated imagery and matching schema image URLs. `scripts/check-build.mjs` verifies actual PNG files and dimensions, matching Twitter metadata, branded tactical figures and social links, and refuses coaching pages using the generic OG image. Both preview and public builds are checked.

### What remains to verify beyond the repository

1. Google Search Console: domain ownership, sitemap submission, index coverage, real impressions/clicks, any selected-canonical conflicts and genuine AI referral signals where available.
2. Bing Webmaster Tools: verification, sitemap, crawl and indexing details. Consider IndexNow or Cloudflare Crawler Hints only if supported and verified.
3. Independent production HTTP verification of `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, image URLs, canonical redirects, HTTP response headers and real screenshots on mobile.
4. PageSpeed Insights/Core Web Vitals: prioritise any real reported LCP, CLS or INP issues. 60 PNG images add build output size; test on mobile, even if they are only loaded on social preview requests.
5. `VideoObject` structured data for the *existing* 3-1-3-1 YouTube explainer could become useful, but do **not** invent the video's upload date or thumbnail; verify these from the real YouTube video first.
6. Image-specific discovery: if needed, create a dedicated diagram-only or format-specific 16:9 and 1:1 image variant in addition to the current 1200 × 630 share cards. A text-forward social sharing preview is not necessarily the best image for Google Images. Do not add dimensions/claims until actual assets exist.
7. Google and AI discovery **cannot be guaranteed** by markup. The public site has readable HTML, source links, authored/reviewed explanations, valid metadata and no AI-only material, which are the main controllable foundations.
