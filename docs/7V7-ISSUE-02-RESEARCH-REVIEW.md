## 1. Editorial approach

This is the second full 7v7 formation issue in How We Play's magazine series. Its structure matches 7v7 Issue 01, **the 2-3-1**, with a starting shape, four principles, role-by-role guidance, an explanation of how the team plays together, and diagrams animated from approved/source-authored player positions.

The contrasting question is **"How can a back three help the team without leaving the striker on their own?"**

The shape uses the goalkeeper and six outfield players:
- GK
- back three: 3 (left), 5 (centre), 2 (right)
- midfield pair: 11 (left), 7 (right)
- striker: 9

This is **3-2-1 outfield** or **1-3-2-1 with the goalkeeper included**. The shirt numbers are illustrative and aren't prescribed by any governing body.

## 2. What the research supports

### Official format facts and examples

**Source:** [England Football Learning, How to play and manage 7v7 games](https://learn.englandfootball.com/articles-and-resources/coaching/resources/2025/How-to-play-and-manage-7v7-games), published 8 December 2025, consulted 22 October 2026.

It confirms:
- From 2026/27, 7v7 is the maximum format for **U10 and U11** in England.
- Seven players including the goalkeeper; 55 × 37 metres recommended pitch; size-three ball; 12 × 6ft goals.
- No offside and no heading. Retreat line and powerplay rules apply; age-specific sideline restarts differ.
- It illustrates **1-2-3-1 and 1-3-2-1** as *examples*, not mandatory formations.
- Player rotation, plenty of game time, autonomy, experimentation and enjoyment are important. An illustrative starting shape must not become a rigid assignment.

### Practitioner discussion and tactical ideas (not official instructions)

**Source:** [World Class Coaching: Train the 3-2-1 for 7v7 games](https://wcctrainingcenter.com/train-the-3-2-1-for-7v7-games/), Matt Carroll, 13 May 2025. Discusses helping outside defenders join the attack at the right moments, communication, midfield positioning and counterattacking opportunities. Treat its proposed solutions as one coach's approach.

**Source:** [England Football Community: 7v7 formations, experiments, experiences](https://community.thefa.com/coaching/f/youth-club-football-forum/138/7v7-formations-experiments-experiences). Grassroots discussion links a 3-2-1 to an opportunity to teach a wide defender's advancing role, but also stresses experimenting and rotating players.

**Source:** [England Football Community: 7v7 game improvement](https://community.thefa.com/coaching/f/youth-club-football-forum/128/7-v-7-game-improvement). Coaches describe problems finding a forward pass when midfielders are too flat or the team is disconnected. Anecdotal practitioner evidence, not a universal assessment of the 3-2-1.

### Editorial interpretation: how it compares with our 2-3-1

| Teaching comparison | 2-3-1 (published Issue 01) | 3-2-1 (proposed Issue 02) |
|---|---|---|
| Starting defensive line | Two defenders | Back three |
| Width in possession | Wide midfielders | Often needs an outside defender to step forward when it's safe |
| Middle support | One central midfielder | Two midfielders need different angles to avoid standing flat |
| Forward | One striker | One striker, with potentially fewer automatic attackers nearby |
| Key trade-off | Wide transition exposes a two-player back line | More cover behind, but possible lack of width and striker isolation |

This comparison is **How We Play's coaching assessment**, not an England Football ranking and not evidence that any formation performs better.

## 3. Four new proposed playing principles

All four are How We Play research-informed editorial interpretations **approved by David Wilson for this issue, as reported by the site owner on 22 October 2026**. They are not official England Football instructions.

1. **Use the back three.** Give the goalkeeper different angles to pass to, rather than three teammates crowded together. **Cue:** “Can our keeper see a pass?”
2. **One goes, two protect.** When safe, an outside defender can provide width; the centre-back and opposite outside defender stay connected. **Cue:** “One goes. Where are the other two?”
3. **One short, one higher.** The two midfielders look for complementary passing angles instead of standing together at the same height. **Cue:** “Can we give two different passes?”
4. **Don't leave nine alone.** A midfielder offers the striker support, with another passing option behind and the team still balanced. **Cue:** “Who's close enough to help nine?”

These are illustrated options, not movement recipes. A run arrow shows a possible choice in a particular picture and does not instruct the same player to repeat it every possession.

## 5. Review questions and future revision checklist

1. **Shape.** Are the back three and midfield pair realistic U10/U11 distances? Are the positions too flat? Do all seven players correctly attack upwards?
2. **Goalkeeper.** Do the illustrated passing angles work with the retreat-line rule, and is it sufficiently clear never to force a short pass?
3. **Width.** Does an outside defender advancing create a clear option without leaving the centre-back alone? Do the two covering defenders position themselves appropriately when the ball is lost?
4. **Midfield.** Does “one short, one higher” allow rotation and sensible help in defence, rather than creating an unhelpful permanent holding/attacking distinction?
5. **Striker.** Do the positions actually support the striker, and should the lesson include different decisions for the striker (show vs threaten depth)?
6. **Defensive transitions.** Is the teaching clear enough about what the midfield pair does when they lose the ball? Are there risks of a back three being too passive?
7. **Diagrams.** Inspect all 18 frames for numerical consistency, ball positions, valid run arrows, space labels, collisions and a sensible wrong/right contrast.
8. **Official rules.** The renderer's 7v7 pitch geometry is labelled **provisional schematic**. Verify pitch markings against the current official mini-soccer rules before calling it a scale drawing. No diagrams may suggest heading or rely on offside.
9. **Developmental focus.** Emphasise getting experience in different roles, meaningful playing time and players making their own choices rather than touchline commands.
