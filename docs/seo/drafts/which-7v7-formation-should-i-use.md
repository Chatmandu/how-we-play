If you've been searching for the *best* seven-a-side formation, here's the honest answer: it depends on the children you're coaching, what they're learning and the way they play together.

The numbers help us draw a starting picture. They don't tell every child exactly where to stand for the next 50 minutes. That is the thinking behind our formation playbooks: a shape, four simple principles and a job each player can understand.

We've published three complete 7v7 guides: **2-3-1**, **3-2-1** and **2-1-2-1**. Here's how they compare, using the strengths and principles in those reviewed guides rather than a league table of “winning” systems.

## Quick comparison

| Shape | How it starts | What our reviewed guide highlights | Explore |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **2-3-1** | Two defenders, three midfielders, one forward | A defensive pair, width from wide midfielders and a central link between the ends | [2-3-1 guide](/formations/7v7/2-3-1/) |
| **3-2-1** | Three defenders, two midfielders, one forward | Three connected defenders, an outside defender who can provide width, and midfield support at different heights | [3-2-1 guide](/formations/7v7/3-2-1/) |
| **2-1-2-1** | Two defenders, a central link, two attacking midfielders and a forward | Passing angles around the ball and a link between defence and attack | [2-1-2-1 guide](/formations/7v7/2-1-2-1/) |

The goalkeeper is the **seventh player** in all three shapes. The numbers describe outfield positions, not a demand that children stay in one zone.

## 2-3-1: a clear midfield line

Our [2-3-1 playbook](/formations/7v7/2-3-1/) starts with two defenders supporting each other, two wide midfielders helping create width, a central midfielder linking play and a striker who can offer depth or come short.

The four reviewed principles are **Give two options**, **Big with it, small without it**, **One presses, one covers**, and **Lose it, find home**. They're built around movement and shared decisions, not children holding a rigid line.

What to discuss with your team: where are the easy options when a defender receives the ball, and how do we stay connected when we lose it? These are questions to explore, not instructions to shout every few seconds.

## 3-2-1: three players across the back

The [3-2-1](/formations/7v7/3-2-1/) adds an extra starting defender. Its reviewed guide emphasises a centre-back and two outside defenders working together, with an outside defender able to offer width while others keep the team connected.

The midfield pair can work at different heights: one offering a shorter passing option, the other supporting further forward. The striker is not meant to be left alone.

Its four principles are **Use the back three**, **One goes, two protect**, **One short, one higher**, and **Don't leave nine alone**. The words focus on relationships between players. Read the diagrams for a clearer picture of what they mean.

This shape is not automatically “defensive”. It can give young players different ways to learn when to support, when to move wide and when to protect space.

## 2-1-2-1: a player linking the lines

The [2-1-2-1](/formations/7v7/2-1-2-1/) places a central link between the two defenders and the attacking midfielders. Our reviewed guide calls it a possible later-stage 7v7 option, **not** a test children must pass. Players should still rotate through different jobs.

It highlights **Link the lines**, **Make passing diamonds**, **Support the striker**, and **Protect the middle**. A team can explore how one player offers a forward angle, how the attacking midfielders connect to the striker and how teammates recover together.

The phrase *passing diamond* can sound complicated to an eight- or ten-year-old. The idea in the guide is much simpler: move into a space where a teammate can find you.

## How do I decide?

Start by watching the children, not by choosing a formation because it won a professional match.

- Can they find a teammate when playing out from their goalkeeper?
- Do they notice the player on the far side?
- Do they give one another options when the ball moves?
- Are they learning different roles rather than being labelled a defender or striker for the season?

There is nothing wrong with testing more than one starting shape. It may show you something about your players that a single fixed formation does not. England Football also encourages children to experience different positions and small-sided formats.

In **England's 2026/27 pathway**, U10 and U11 play 7v7 as the maximum matchday format. In the **USA**, 7v7 generally aligns to different age groups and build-out-line rules. Our [US coaching guide](/coaching-in-the-usa/) explains why the age numbers aren't interchangeable.

The important point is what the children learn, not whether their teamsheet looks perfect before kick-off. Choose a starting picture, teach a couple of clear decisions, and let them play.
