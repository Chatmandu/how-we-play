# 9v9 · Issue 03 — The 2-2-3-1

**Editorial draft 0.2 · PRIVATE · Exploratory proposal for Niall Walsh and David Wilson**

**Status:** Exploratory and unreviewed. This document expands `content/formations/9v9/2-2-3-1.private.json` (research draft 0.1). No material in this file is authorised for public coaching advice. The reviewer should be free to reject the system, alter its notation or suggest a different formation entirely.

**Issue proposition:** *Two central defenders. Two wing-backs. Three midfielders. One striker. A starting shape whose responsibilities change as the ball moves.*

## Cover / standfirst

The 2-2-3-1 gives the team a different picture: two central defenders behind two wide wing-backs, three midfielders to connect play and a forward leading the line. That may help players explore width, support and recovery. It also creates difficult questions: how do the wing-backs get back, who protects the middle when one goes forward and how can two centre-backs cope when several opponents attack quickly?

This issue should **not** recommend the formation until a qualified reviewer establishes that its explanation is sound and appropriate for the age group. It is currently a teaching hypothesis.

## Chapter 1 — Starting shape

**Outfield notation:** 2-2-3-1. **Goalkeeper-first notation:** 1-2-2-3-1.

- **GK:** goalkeeper.
- **4 and 5:** central defenders.
- **3 and 2:** wide wing-backs positioned ahead of the defenders in the starting illustration.
- **11, 6 and 8:** three midfielders, with 6 central.
- **9:** forward.

**Player-facing draft:** “The formation is where we begin. It isn't where we have to stand. Our wing-backs help us attack but must also help protect our goal.”

**Possible opportunities** identified in the existing research draft: width from wing-backs; passing relationships among three midfielders; a high forward; experience in roles involving attacking and defending.

**Significant possible costs:** room behind advancing wing-backs; potential overloads against the two central defenders; an isolated 9; crowded central passing lanes; recovery distances that may be too demanding for some players.

**First reviewer decision:** Is the description 2-2-3-1 genuinely clear and honest, or would 2-4-1-1 or 2-3-2-1 better represent the intended relationships? If the initial notation is misleading, correct the system before creating new diagrams.

## Chapter 2 — Four proposed playing principles

### 01 · Two stay connected

**Suggested message:** “Our centre-backs defend together.”

**Player's question:** “Am I close enough to help?”

The existing draft suggests that defenders 4 and 5 need to recognise pressure and cover as a pair. Neither can assume the other will handle every threat.

**Diagram proposal, for tactical review only:**
1. A neutral view of 4 and 5 with the ball in a central area.
2. A possible risk in which both defenders chase one opponent and leave another route available.
3. A possible pressure-and-cover response, with the exact positioning and opponent movement selected by David.

**Must verify:** Opponent numbers, distance between defenders and the most useful action when the ball is played into the channel. Do not suggest that two stay fixed together in all situations.

### 02 · Width with cover

**Suggested message:** “Wing-backs go when someone can protect behind.”

**Player's question:** “If I go, who covers?”

The starting idea is that wing-backs can offer width, but the team must understand what changes when one advances. The draft does not establish a definitive covering pattern; that is an explicit reviewer decision.

**Diagram proposal, for tactical review only:**
1. The right wing-back 2 considers supporting play outside.
2. Show the potential space behind 2 if possession changes.
3. Show the reviewed support and covering decision, once David defines which midfielder or defender protects the channel.

**Must verify:** Whether left and right can be mirrored, whether both wing-backs may ever advance, and which responsibilities are manageable for U12–U13 players.

### 03 · Someone protects the middle

**Suggested message:** “Don't leave the route to our goal open.”

**Player's question:** “Who is stopping the pass through?”

When teammates move to help near the ball, a central player needs to notice danger. The existing draft associates this protective question particularly with 6 but cautions against making 6 a fixed defensive specialist.

**Diagram proposal, for tactical review only:**
1. Possession with the wing-back offering width.
2. A possible turnover showing the central route exposed.
3. A reviewed protective response involving 6 and the nearest other midfielder.

**Must verify:** Whether the 6 can provide adequate cover in the proposed shape; whether the other two midfielders should be positioned more narrowly in different phases; how to avoid interfering with wing-back lanes.

### 04 · Lose it? Find home

**Existing draft title:** “Wing-back recovery”

**Draft touchline cue:** “Press, protect or recover: which is my job?”

**Existing explanation:** “Find home” does **not** mean blindly running back to a painted position and ignoring an opportunity to delay play.

The existing `wb-recovery` diagram in the private formation JSON is a three-stage sequence showing a turnover, an attempt to delay and a recovery picture. Keep this as a proposal, not a verified instruction.

**Critical reviewer decision:** Should the headline become “Press or recover” or “First job, next job”? The current wording could inadvertently discourage useful immediate pressure, so don't lock it in without review.

**Must verify:** Recovery distances, opponent pressure, likely passing routes and whether the proposed action matches the developmental abilities of the players.

## Chapter 3 — A job for every player

The descriptions below are grounded in the existing draft's `roleSummary`. They are **not** complete, approved role pages.

| Player(s) | Starting role | Working headline | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|---|
| GK | Goalkeeper | See danger early | What could happen if we lose this pass? |
| 4 and 5 | Central defenders | Protect each other | Am I the pressure or the cover? |
| 3 and 2 | Wing-backs | Two jobs, one decision | If I leave this space, who has it? |
| 6 | Central midfielder | Our connection and protection | Who is protecting the middle? |
| 11 and 8 | Interior midfielders | Make angles, not crowds | Am I helping the player on the ball or making it crowded? |
| 9 | Striker | Give us a way forward | Can I receive, turn, or help someone run? |

**Position-page plan:** One neutral role highlight, one carefully bounded situation and a player-friendly question. For wing-backs, a reviewed attacking-to-defending sequence will be essential; for centre-backs, include a realistic overload scenario only after coaching sign-off.

## Chapter 4 — Playing together

### When we have the ball

The wing-backs may create width while 11, 6 and 8 connect passing options. The striker needs a nearby relationship so the high position doesn't become isolated. **Width with cover** and **Someone protects the middle** must be taught together.

**Proposed illustration:** Ball on the right, right wing-back providing width, 6 and 8 connecting centrally, the opposite side balancing. Ask David which player should provide cover and which combinations are realistic.

### When the opposition have the ball

The team needs a clear decision on first pressure, central protection and the recovery responsibilities of players who started higher up the pitch. **Two stay connected** and **Lose it? Find home** are linked, but the coach needs to settle the actual movements.

**Proposed illustration:** An opposition break towards the wide channel after our attack ends. Compare a disorganised picture and an age-appropriate reviewed solution. The exact opponents, run arrows and defensive line must be approved before drawing.
