# 9v9 · Issue 02 — The 3-3-2

**Editorial draft 0.2 · PRIVATE · For Niall Walsh and David Wilson to review**

**Status:** Unreviewed coaching proposals, not suitable for publication. This document expands the existing `content/formations/9v9/3-3-2.private.json` research draft 0.1. It does **not** claim a single universal way to play 3-3-2 or reproduce an approved team playbook. Any new scenario described here is a *proposed illustration* requiring tactical review.

**Issue proposition:** *Three protect. Three connect. Two work together.*

## Cover / standfirst

Nine players, three clear outfield lines and two forwards who can help each other. The 3-3-2 is a straightforward starting picture for 9v9, but it only works when the lines stay connected. The midfield cannot leave the back three isolated, and the forwards need to offer different options rather than standing together waiting for a pass.

This issue introduces our suggested shape, four easy-to-remember principles and a job for each player. It is a starting point to adapt to the players in front of you, not a formation that stays fixed throughout a match.

## Chapter 1 — Starting shape

**Goalkeeper-first notation:** 1-3-3-2; **outfield notation:** 3-3-2.

- **GK** behind the back three.
- **3, 5, 2:** outside defender, centre-back, outside defender.
- **11, 8, 7:** wide midfielder, central midfielder, wide midfielder.
- **9, 10:** two forwards, sharing the work in and out of possession.

**Player-facing explanation:** “Our back three give us cover. Our midfield three link both ends. Our two forwards give us somewhere to play and press together. We move as the ball moves.”

**What this gives us (initial hypotheses):** a recognisable three-player defence, a midfield line that can offer passing angles, a natural partner for each forward and roles that may be readily understood by players moving up from a smaller format.

**It also asks a lot of us:** wide midfielders may need to support both ends; 8 can be outnumbered centrally; the forwards can become disconnected; the back three need help if the opposition commit extra attackers.

**Review before diagram production:** Should 8 start centrally as a relatively flat three, or sometimes sit deeper? Show a neutral starting shape without presenting a single fixed answer.

## Chapter 2 — Four playing principles

### 01 · Middle first

**Say it:** “Protect the route to our goal before chasing wide.”

**Touchline question:** “Press or cover?”

The team should know who is dealing with the ball and who is protecting the middle. A wide defender or midfielder should not leave a direct route to goal open simply because the ball moves outside.

**Proposed diagram sequence (needs coaching sign-off):**
1. **The choice:** Ball on the opponent's right. Show 8 and the back three; identify central space to protect.
2. **Too many chase:** An illustrative mistake where multiple players move towards the same ball and a central passing lane opens.
3. **Press and cover:** One nearby player puts pressure on the ball while teammates keep the central route guarded.

**Key review question:** Which player applies pressure in the illustrated phase, and how does that change against two central midfielders or three high attackers? Do not draw definitive movements until the coach selects the scenario.

### 02 · Ball side wide, far side in

**Say it:** “Give width near the ball; the far midfielder comes inside to help.”

**Touchline question:** “Stay wide or tuck in?”

With the ball on one side, the nearby wide midfielder can offer width. The opposite wide midfielder may help the centre by narrowing their position. This is a decision, not a command to permanently abandon the far touchline.

**Proposed diagram sequence (needs coaching sign-off):**
1. **Ball on the right:** 7 offers a wide option near the ball; 11 considers a narrower supporting position.
2. **Supporting triangles:** Show how 8 relates to the ball-side midfielder; coach to specify exactly which defender joins.
3. **Switch available:** The far-side player recognises when staying wider may be the better option, avoiding an absolute rule.

**Review checkpoint:** When should the far side stay wide? Can children spot that cue without being asked to memorise too many exceptions?

### 03 · One comes, one goes

**Say it:** “Our two forwards shouldn't make the same run.”

**Touchline question:** “Offer feet or threaten behind?”

This is the most developed part of the existing research draft. One of the two forwards can move towards the ball to offer a pass into feet while the other gives the defence something else to consider. The responsibilities can reverse: 9 comes and 10 goes, or 10 comes and 9 goes.

**Existing diagram:** `one-comes` in the 3-3-2 private JSON has three frames: same line, 9 comes/10 goes, 10 comes/9 goes.

**Important:** Its current offside overlays are explicitly marked TO CHECK. Confirm the line, players and timing against current Law 11 and target competitions before the scene is approved.

**Coach discussion:** How do 8 and the wide midfielders remain close enough to provide the next pass? What should the second forward do when there is no space to run behind?

### 04 · Go together, recover together

**Say it:** “If one presses, others close the next pass; if beaten, recover as a unit.”

**Touchline question:** “Are teammates close enough to help?”

The first player cannot do all the work. Pressing is useful when nearby players are positioned to stop the easy next option. If the press is beaten, the team needs a simple recovery decision instead of everybody chasing the same opponent.

**Proposed diagram sequence (needs coaching sign-off):**
1. **An isolated press:** The nearest forward presses but the other forward and midfield have left an easy route through.
2. **Partners close options:** Coach chooses who screens or delays the inside pass and who provides cover; avoid asserting a universal pressing trigger.
3. **Recover together:** Show the nearest useful defensive action after the first challenge is bypassed.

**Review checkpoint:** Is the two-forward press sufficiently supported by the midfield? What is a clear, age-appropriate trigger to go?

## Chapter 3 — A job for every player

Each short role page should share: a starting position, the primary job with and without the ball, one player-friendly question, a diagram and the principles it connects to. The jobs below paraphrase **only** the existing draft's role summaries. More precise movements require approval.

| Player(s) | Role | Headline | Ask yourself |
|---|---|---|---|
| GK | Goalkeeper | Start, see, speak | Who is free, and what happens if I pass there? |
| 5 | Centre-back | See the whole picture | If I step in, who's behind me? |
| 3 and 2 | Outside defenders | Defend, support, cover | Am I pressing or covering? |
| 8 | Central midfielder | Connect both ends | Can they play through us if I move? |
| 11 and 7 | Wide midfielders | Width and hard work | Should I be wide or inside right now? |
| 9 and 10 | Two forwards | Partners, not passengers | Where is my partner going? |

**Role-page diagram plan:** One neutral shape with the role highlighted; one proposed decision example from the relevant named principle. No invented set-piece assignments.

## Chapter 4 — Playing together

### When we have the ball

Bring the wide players, central midfielder and two forwards into the same picture. Connect **Ball side wide, far side in** to **One comes, one goes**. The player on the ball should be able to see more than one useful decision, with the two forwards offering different problems for defenders.

**Diagram for review:** A right-sided possession example showing 7, 8, 9 and 10, with 11 supporting from the far side. The coach must approve the positions and passing lanes; do not imply players must make one rehearsed move.

### When the opposition have the ball

Connect **Middle first** to **Go together, recover together**. Players need to know who can press, who protects the middle and how the group recovers when the opposition play around or through the first challenge.

**Diagram for review:** A ball-side pressure decision that includes the support roles behind the nearest pressing player.
